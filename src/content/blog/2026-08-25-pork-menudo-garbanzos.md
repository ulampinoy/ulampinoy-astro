    - steps:
        - "**Marinate the pork and liver:** Combine the pork, liver, onion, garlic, soy sauce, calamansi juice, and pepper in a bowl. Set aside for at least 20 minutes, or up to overnight in the fridge."
        - "**Brown the meat:** Heat the oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat. Add the pork and liver together with all of the marinade and aromatics. Cook, stirring now and then, until the juices have mostly evaporated and the meat is lightly browned, about 10–12 minutes."
        - "**Build the sauce:** Stir in the tomato paste (if using) and cook for 1 minute. Add the tomato sauce, water, and bay leaves. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat, cover, and simmer for 40 minutes, or until the pork is fork-tender. Add a splash of water if the sauce gets too thick."

If there is one dish that tells you a Filipino party is about to start, it is a big, steaming _kaldero_ of **pork menudo**. It is on the table at every _fiesta_, birthday, and _handaan_—cubes of tender pork and savory liver, sweet carrots, soft potatoes, all swimming in a thick, glossy tomato sauce.

_This is our fiesta-style version, with one ingredient that turns it from everyday ulam into something special: **garbanzos**._

![Easy Pork Menudo, fiesta style, with garbanzos simmering in the pot](/.netlify/images?url=/images/pork-menudo-fiesta-pot.jpg)

## Fiesta-Style Pork Menudo

Menudo looks like a complicated, all-day dish, but it is really just one pot and a bit of patience. Here is why you will keep coming back to this recipe:

- **Fork-tender pork.** A long, gentle simmer melts the pork until it falls apart on your spoon.
- **A thick, savory sauce.** No watery menudo here. The sauce clings to every piece and soaks right into your rice.
- **Garbanzos for texture.** They add a nutty bite and a creaminess that makes the dish feel festive.
- **Even better tomorrow.** It is perfect for parties, potlucks, and _baon_ because it improves overnight.

![Pork menudo in a white serving bowl](/.netlify/images?url=/images/menudo-serving-bowl.jpg)

## How to Make Pork Menudo

There are just four things that make a great menudo:

- **A quick marinade.** Soy sauce, calamansi, pepper, onion, and garlic season the pork and liver from the inside.
- **Proper browning.** Cook off the meat juices before adding the sauce for a deeper, meatier flavor.
- **A slow simmer.** The pork needs time to turn tender before the vegetables go in.
- **Smart timing.** Potatoes, carrots, and garbanzos go in near the end so they hold their shape, and the bell peppers go in last so they stay bright.

![Pork and liver marinating with onion, garlic, soy sauce, and calamansi](/.netlify/images?url=/images/menudo-pork-liver-marinating.jpg)

## What Pork Cut to Use

**Pork kasim (shoulder)** is our first pick. It has just enough fat to stay juicy through the long simmer. **Liempo (belly)** gives you a richer, more indulgent menudo. Leaner cuts like _pigue_ (leg) work too, but keep an eye on them so they do not dry out.

## What You Need

Most of the ingredients are pantry staples, but here are the ones that matter most:

- **Liver.** It is what makes menudo _menudo_. Pork liver is classic, and chicken liver is milder. Not a fan? A spoonful of liver spread mashed into the sauce gives the same savory depth.
- **Tomato sauce.** It is the base of the sauce. A little tomato paste makes it redder and richer.
- **Garbanzos.** Jarred or canned garbanzos are the easy route. Just drain and rinse them. If you cook them from dried, soak them overnight and boil them until creamy before they go into the pot.
- **Potatoes, carrots, and bell peppers.** They are the classic vegetables for color and sweetness. We use both green and red bell peppers.

![Potatoes, carrots, and green and red bell peppers for menudo](/.netlify/images?url=/images/menudo-vegetables.jpg)

## Why Garbanzos?

![A jar of cooked garbanzos](/.netlify/images?url=/images/menudo-garbanzos-jar.jpg)

Garbanzos are a nod to menudo's Spanish roots, where chickpeas are a staple in stews like _cocido_. In Filipino fiesta cooking, they have become a sign of a _handaan_-style menudo. They add a subtle nuttiness and a soft bite that goes perfectly with the rich tomato sauce. And they stretch the dish so it feeds a crowd.

![Garbanzos stirred into the simmering menudo](/.netlify/images?url=/images/menudo-garbanzos-pot.jpg)

## What to Serve with Pork Menudo

- **Steamed white rice.** This is non-negotiable! Here is [how to cook perfect rice](/blog/2023-03-27-how-to-cook-rice) every time.
- **Pandesal.** It is great for mopping up leftover sauce the next morning.
- **Something fresh and sour.** Pair it with _atchara_ (pickled papaya) or a simple tomato-and-onion salad to cut the richness.
- **More party food.** Add [lumpia shanghai](/blog/2023-04-09-lumpia-shanghai) and [pancit](/blog/2023-04-10-pancit-guisado-special) and you have a complete fiesta spread.

Menudo is a very forgiving dish. If the sauce is too thick, add a splash of water. If it is too thin, simmer it uncovered for a few minutes. If you are not sure it is done, give it ten more minutes. Your family will not complain. _Kain na!_
